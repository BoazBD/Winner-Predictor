Where is the money on Polymarket soccer?

Tested on 13 million real Polymarket trades from 16,136 soccer games (Sep 2024 – Nov 2026), including trades during and after the games. Written 20 Nov 2026. No real money was used.

Short answer. Predicting how the price will move, then selling before kickoff, does not work — prices before the game don't trend, and the fee eats any small move. But the trades show one clear, repeated pattern: people who buy longshots lose heavily, and whoever sells to them wins. The best strategy found is to be that seller: place limit orders against extreme longshots (3–8¢) before the game. It won 97% of its bets and made about +3.6% per bet in every test period — but it does depend on game results. Two truly result-free trades also exist (arbitrage with Winner, and buying winning shares after the final whistle), but both are tiny.

1. The pattern: longshot buyers lose

For every share, I took the first real purchase in the last 24 hours before kickoff and checked how it ended. Fee included.

Buyers of 3–8¢ shares lost about half their money in each of the three periods (−45%, −59%, −68%). Buyers of 8–15¢ shares lost 12–22%. In the middle (30–85¢), buyers lose roughly the fee, so the price is fair. Longshots are overpriced because people like cheap lottery tickets.

2. Best strategy: be the seller of longshots

Exact rules (strategy B)
  1. When: 6 hours before kickoff, look at every soccer game on Polymarket.
  2. What: any outcome priced between 3¢ and 8¢ (a big underdog, or a draw in a very one-sided game).
  3. Order: place a limit order to buy the "No" share of that outcome at (100¢ − its current price). Example: outcome at 6¢ → bid 94¢ for "No". This is a resting limit order, so there is no fee.
  4. If nobody fills it by kickoff: cancel. No trade, no cost.
  5. If filled: hold to the end of the game. "No" pays 100¢ unless the longshot actually wins.
  6. Size: the same small amount on every bet (e.g. 100 shares ≈ $94). Never more on one game — a loss costs about 15 wins.

A fill is counted only when a real trade went through our price (strict). Rules were chosen on games before July 2026, then checked on July–September, then run once on October–November.

13 of the last 15 months were profitable. A typical recent month has 40–65 bets and earns about $100–290 at 100 shares per bet, using roughly $1,000–2,000 of capital at a time (money is tied up for less than a day per bet). Half of the fills had 900+ shares of real buying behind them, so bets of a few hundred dollars are realistic; much bigger is not.

Can we close before kickoff and avoid the game?

No. Buying the position back 5 minutes before kickoff turns the profit into a loss: -1.1%, -1.2% and -1.7% in the three periods. The profit comes from the longshot not winning, not from a price move.

Risks

  • It depends on results. About 1 bet in 35 loses, and a loss wipes out about 15 wins. Several losses close together are possible: the worst months (Oct 2025, Apr 2026) had 3 losses and ended slightly negative.
  • Our orders weren't in the real book. I assume we get filled when a real trade crosses our price. If others are ahead of us in the queue we fill less often — fewer bets, same quality.
  • Fill rates are falling (76% → 56% → 51%), which suggests more sellers are competing. The edge can shrink.
  • Sample size. 587 bets with 16 losses. The 95% range for the true return is +2.1% to +4.9% per bet.
  • The Oct–Nov period was already looked at in earlier studies (for other strategies). A fully clean test is a live paper-trade from today.

3. Truly result-free trades (real, but small)

a) Arbitrage: Winner vs Polymarket risk-free, rare

Winner is slow. Sometimes Polymarket moves and, for a few minutes, betting an outcome at Winner and buying "No" on the same outcome at Polymarket locks a profit whatever the result. Checked against real Polymarket trades and Winner odds that were live before and after.

  • Found in 67 of about 16,700 games (0.4%). Recently about 8 games a month.
  • Typical locked profit: 1.5% of the money put in.
  • Usually 45–75 minutes before kickoff (line-up news), and the window lasts only a few minutes.
  • With up to $1,000 per game: about $48 a month.
  • Needs a single bet at Winner placed within minutes. Check Winner's rules on single bets, limits and tax first.

b) Buy the winner after the final whistle near risk-free, crowded

After a game ends, holders who don't want to wait for the payout sell winning shares at 99.9¢. A resting bid at 99.9¢ earns 0.1¢ per share, with no fee.

  • From 150 minutes after kickoff: $67M was sold this way, and only $632 of it went to a share that lost.
  • Profit: 0.1% per round. Recently about $72k a day is sold this way in these games, so the whole pie is about $72 a day, shared between all bidders (mostly bots, first in line gets filled).
  • Safe, but realistically a few dollars a day.

c) Late-game lottery tickets small, needs speed

In the last minutes, sellers of 1–2¢ shares earned +0.9%, +0.4% and +0.6% on their capital in the three periods. Positive every time, but a late goal turns a 1¢ share into a full loss, and faster bots pull their orders first.

4. What does not work

IdeaResult
Predict the price move, sell before kickoffno After a move of 3¢+ in the last 1–3 hours, the next move averages 0.0–0.5¢. A round trip costs about 3¢ in fees and spread.
Buy heavy favourites (85–97¢)no As a fixed rule: −0.3%, +0.3%, +2.0% in the three periods — not reliable.
Buy near-certain shares during the gameno In-play prices are accurate; buyers lose about the fee.
Sell 8–30¢ outcomesno About 0%.
Follow Winner moves; buy low and limit-sellno Earlier studies: −2% to −8%.

The common thread: on Polymarket, people who take prices lose and people who post limit orders win. Every profitable result here is on the posting side.

5. Recommendation

  1. Paper-trade strategy B live for 4–6 weeks in our existing system (it already reads Polymarket every 3 hours). This gives a clean test and real fill data.
  2. If it holds, start with small real size (about $50–100 per bet) and fixed stakes.
  3. Treat the arbitrage as an optional alert ("Winner is stale vs Polymarket now"), not as income.

How this was tested

  • 13.0M real trades (pre-match, in-play, post-game) for 16,136 games with clean results; minute-by-minute prices; Winner odds every 15 minutes.
  • Three periods: before 1 Jul 2026 (choose rules), Jul–Sep 2026 (check), Oct–18 Nov 2026 (final test).
  • Ranges are 95% bootstrap intervals, resampling whole games.
