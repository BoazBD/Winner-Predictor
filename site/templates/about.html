How it works

This site tests whether betting models built on Winner's odds can make money on Polymarket. Everything here is a simulation: bets are recorded, not placed.

Every 3 hours

  1. Winner odds. A scraper saves all of Winner's odds every 15 minutes. The predictor reads the last three weeks of odds for every open game.
  2. Polymarket prices. It reads every upcoming soccer game on Polymarket with a home / draw / away market: buy and sell prices, available money and the taker fee.
  3. Matching. Winner lists games in Hebrew, in Israel time. A Winner game is matched to the Polymarket game with the same kickoff (within 12 minutes) whose team names agree, compared by sound and by English name. Only matched games are used.
  4. Models. Each model estimates the chance of a home win, draw and away win.
  5. Paper bets. For every outcome, expected value = model probability × Polymarket odds − 1, where the odds include Polymarket's fee (5% × p × (1 − p) per share). The first time it is above 3%, the outcome is bought for 1 unit — once per model and outcome. Wide spreads, prices under 3¢ or over 97¢, and large disagreements with Polymarket (likely a wrong match) are skipped.
  6. Results. When a game ends the bet is settled from Winner's results: a win pays odds − 1, a loss costs 1. A bet with no result three days after kickoff (postponed game) is void.

How to read the results

  • CLV (closing-line value) compares the Polymarket price at kickoff with what the bet cost. Prices at kickoff are the market's best estimate, so bets that keep beating them have a real edge. CLV answers that question after a few hundred bets; profit needs thousands.
  • P&L and ROI swing a lot by luck. A short run of wins or losses means little.
  • The first three models get their probabilities from Winner's own odds, and Polymarket's prices are usually sharper, so their bets are expected to lose slowly unless the data shows otherwise.
